# Stock SIPs

## stock-sip-overview
title: How stock SIPs work
screen: stocksip
roles: Ops maker, Ops checker, Risk manager, Compliance officer
ask: stock sip; equity sip; sip in shares; sip in stocks; share sip; etf sip; buy shares every month; monthly share purchase; how do stock sips work; systematic investment in shares
related: stock-sip-rules, stock-sip-register, stock-sip-orders, stock-sip-failures, stock-sip-topups

A stock SIP is a client's standing instruction to buy a share or ETF for a fixed **amount** or a fixed **number of shares**, every trading day, every week on a weekday, or every month on a day. No exchange or depository runs it: it is the firm's own feature, and Ledgerline does not place orders itself.

1. At the firm's **placement time** on each trading day, Ledgerline works out every instalment due: an instalment falling on a holiday moves to the next trading day.
2. The price is the last traded price (or the last close). For an amount, the quantity is the amount divided by the price, **rounded down**: fractional shares cannot be bought, so an amount below one share fails.
3. The scrip is checked again, and the client's **free funds** (ledger before the day's bills, less margin not covered by collateral, uncleared cheques and approved payouts, less the day's earlier SIP orders, plus a top-up on its way) must cover the order at its limit or protected price **with charges**. Otherwise the instalment fails: there are no partial orders.
4. The orders go to the OMS (a basket file the dealer uploads, or the Symphony XTS order API). Trades come back in the day's trade files, are matched to the orders, and are billed, noted and lotted for capital gains like any other trade. Contract notes go out the usual way.
5. Orders not executed by the end of the day **lapse**. A SIP stops after the firm's number of failed instalments in a row.

The client gets a message when an order is placed, bought, not bought, or when the SIP stops.

## stock-sip-rules
title: Decide the rules for stock SIPs
screen: stocksip
roles: Compliance officer
ask: stock sip settings; sip order type; is a sip order an algo order; sip algo tagging; sip placement time; sip market protection; sip limit order; sip consent wording; switch on stock sips; sip brokerage
related: stock-sip-overview, stock-sip-lists

The compliance officer sets the rules on the Stock SIPs screen, under **Settings**:

1. **Order type**: limit (at the last price plus the protection, rounded down to the tick) or market (with the protection as market protection). SEBI's retail algo framework applies to all brokers from 1 April 2026 and treats any order generated by automated execution logic as an algo order. Whether scheduled SIP orders are algo orders is the firm's decision: if they are tagged, give the algo ID the exchange approved. NSE does not accept market orders from algos, so tagged SIP orders must be limit orders.
2. **Placement time**, between 09:15 and 15:00.
3. **Scrips allowed**: only the firm's list (for example the top 250 by market value and ETFs), or any equity share or ETF.
4. **Failures in a row** after which a SIP stops (5 is common) and the **minimum order**.
5. **Brokerage**: the channel of the brokerage plans SIP orders are billed under (map the SIP dealer terminal to that channel in Brokerage setup), or a brokerage per instalment.
6. The **standing instruction** clients agree to. Changing its wording makes a new version; each SIP keeps the wording, version and time it was given with.

Changes are recorded with who decided the order type, time and algo tagging.

## stock-sip-register
title: Register, pause or cancel a client's stock SIP
screen: stocksip
roles: Ops maker, Ops checker
ask: register stock sip; start a stock sip for a client; client wants a stock sip; stock sip standing instruction form; pause stock sip; resume stock sip; cancel stock sip; skip stock sip instalment; stop stock sip
related: stock-sip-overview, stock-sip-failures

Clients start, pause, skip and cancel their own SIPs in the client portal (Stock SIPs), agreeing to the standing instruction shown.

For a client who gave the instruction on a **signed form** or a **recorded call**:

1. On the Stock SIPs screen, click **New SIP**, give the client code, scrip, amount or number of shares, frequency and day, start and (optional) end.
2. **Review** shows the standing instruction, the price, the shares it buys today and the funds needed. Record how the client gave it and the form number or call reference.
3. **Pause** stops instalments until a date or until resumed; **Skip next** skips one instalment; **Cancel** revokes the standing instruction (a new SIP needs a new one).

The account must be active, not blocked or inactive, with the cash segment open.

## stock-sip-orders
title: Send the day's SIP orders to the OMS
screen: stocksip
roles: Ops maker, Ops checker, Risk manager
ask: sip basket file; send sip orders to the oms; upload sip orders in odin; sip bulk order file; sip orders in nest; xts sip orders; sip orders not sent; basket file layout for our oms; sip order reference
related: stock-sip-failures, stock-sip-xts

With the **basket file** (the default):

1. After the placement time, open Stock SIPs → **Today**. The instalments are worked out (or click **Work out instalments now**).
2. Click **Make basket file**: it downloads, and the orders are marked placed. The dealer uploads it in the OMS as a basket (bulk) order.
3. If it could not be uploaded, click **Not uploaded** on the file: its orders wait again and a new file can be made.

OMS basket layouts differ. The generic layout is `config/formats/stocksip-basket.json`; for your OMS, copy it as `stocksip-basket-<oms>.json` with the OMS's column names and order (keep each column's key), then choose it under Settings.

With **Symphony XTS**, the orders are placed through the XTS order API as soon as they are worked out; an order XTS refuses keeps waiting with the reason. Other OMS APIs are not public, so those firms use the basket file.

## stock-sip-xts
title: Connect Symphony XTS for SIP orders
screen: connections
roles: Admin
ask: xts api for sip; symphony xts order entry; xts app key; xts secret key; xts instrument id; place sip orders by api
related: stock-sip-orders, stock-sip-lists

1. On [[connections|Connections]], open **Symphony XTS order entry (stock SIPs)**: the XTS address of your broker or Symphony, and the interactive API's app key and secret key. The login must be a **dealer** login: an investor (client) login cannot place orders for clients.
2. Upload the OMS instrument IDs (Exchange, Symbol, InstrumentID: XTS's exchangeInstrumentID) under Stock SIPs → Scrip lists.
3. Under Stock SIPs → Settings, choose **Symphony XTS order API**.

Each order carries its SIP reference as the order's unique identifier, so the dealer can find it in the order book.

## stock-sip-failures
title: Why a SIP instalment was not bought
screen: stocksip
roles: Ops maker, Ops checker
ask: sip instalment failed; stock sip not bought; sip lapsed; sip insufficient funds; sip stopped after failures; sip check fills; record sip fill; sip order not executed; sip not sent
related: stock-sip-orders, stock-sip-overview

The Failures tab lists the last 45 days with the reason:

- **Failed**: not enough free funds for the order and its charges, the amount buys less than one share, the scrip is not allowed, or the account cannot buy. Counted against the SIP.
- **Lapsed**: placed but not executed by the end of the day (Ledgerline waits for the exchange's trade file). Counted against the SIP.
- **Not sent**: never handed to the OMS on the day. The firm's side: not counted against the client.
- **Check fills**: the client has buy trades in the scrip that do not match the order's quantity. Check the OMS order book and **Record fill** (0 if not executed).

Trades are matched by the OMS order ID when the trade file carries it, then by the algo ID when SIP orders are tagged, then by client, scrip, date and quantity. A single smaller order is a part fill.

## stock-sip-lists
title: Keep the scrip lists for stock SIPs
screen: stocksip
roles: Compliance officer, Risk manager
ask: scrips allowed for sip; asm list for sip; restricted scrips for sip; top 250 list for sip; t2t scrips in sip; trade for trade sip; etf list for sip
related: stock-sip-rules

On Stock SIPs → **Scrip lists**, upload each as a CSV with a header row:

1. **Scrips allowed**: Symbol and/or ISIN (optional Exchange, Name, Category). Used when the rule is "only the firm's list".
2. **Restricted scrips**: Symbol and/or ISIN with the Reason. Upload the exchanges' ASM and GSM lists here whenever they change. These are never bought, even for an existing SIP.
3. **OMS instrument IDs**, for the XTS order API.

Trade-for-trade series (NSE BE and BZ; BSE groups T, TS, XT and Z) and suspended scrips, from the security masters, are never bought.

## stock-sip-topups
title: Top up SIP shortfalls by UPI AutoPay or e-mandate
screen: connections
roles: Admin, Compliance officer, Ops checker
ask: sip top up; topup for stock sip; upi autopay for sip; e-mandate for sip; autopay sip shortfall; pre-debit notice for sip; cashfree subscription; auto debit before sip
related: stock-sip-overview, record-receipt

With top-ups on, a client's shortfall for an instalment is debited from **their own registered bank account** the day before, so the money is in the ledger on the day.

1. On [[connections|Connections]], set up **Stock SIP top-ups** (Cashfree Subscriptions: client ID and client secret). Cashfree must settle into the **client money bank account**, never the firm's own. Give Cashfree the notice address `/hooks/<firm>/stocksip/cashfree`.
2. The compliance officer switches **top-ups** on under Stock SIPs → Settings.
3. The client sets up a mandate in the portal (or staff create one and send the client the authorisation link); it is registered on the client's registered bank account.
4. Two days before each instalment day, at the time set, Ledgerline raises a debit of the shortfall for the next day: at most **₹15,000** (RBI's limit for e-mandate debits without additional authentication), with notice at least 24 hours before (Cashfree's and the firm's message). A larger shortfall is not debited at all.
5. When Cashfree reports the debit, it is credited to the client's ledger as a receipt against the gateway receivable, cleared by the gateway settlement on Receipts & payouts. A debit on its way counts towards the instalment's funds.

The UPI block facility for trading is not used for SIPs yet.
